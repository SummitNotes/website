You can take AI notes from an in-person conversation or a phone call played through a speaker near your Mac. This guide covers what the Mac can hear and the limits of that setup; it does not promise direct recording of cellular calls on an iPhone. Obtain any required consent before recording.

## The conversations outside scheduled calls

Many important conversations never get a video link:

- **In-person meetings.** The investor meeting. The client pitch. The 1:1 with your manager.

- **Impromptu discussions.** Someone pulls you aside after a meeting, and a quick sync turns into a 45-minute strategy session. No calendar invite, no video link.

- **Phone calls.** A prospect or client calls while you are at your desk, and the call stays on the phone.

For these conversations, many people fall back to handwritten notes or try to remember everything afterwards.

## How note-takers approach them

Many meeting assistants are built around joining a video call as a participant: a bot joins your Zoom or Google Meet and receives the audio stream. That works well when there is a call to join, and some services also offer mobile or desktop recording for conversations that happen elsewhere. Check what a specific product supports, where it processes audio, and whether recordings are uploaded before you rely on it for in-person or phone conversations.

## Phone calls: the forgotten use case

If you take business calls on your phone, you've probably wished for automatic notes.

Summit runs on your Mac, so it works with what the Mac can hear:

- **Speakerphone near your Mac.** Put the call on speaker and keep the phone close to the Mac. The microphone picks up both sides; the other person's audio quality depends on the phone's speaker volume and the room.

- **Call from your Mac.** If you take the call in FaceTime Audio or another calling app on the Mac, Summit can capture the system audio directly.

Summit does not record cellular calls on the iPhone itself. If the call stays on the phone without a speaker near your Mac, Summit will not hear it.

The point is: you're not limited to scheduled video calls. Audio that reaches your Mac can become searchable, summarized meeting notes.

## When offline mode makes sense

Some scenarios where local recording beats cloud bots:

**Sensitive conversations.** Client discussions under NDA. Personnel issues. Competitive strategy. Some conversations shouldn't exist on third-party servers.

**No reliable internet.** Conference venues with overloaded WiFi. International travel. Rural client sites. Cloud services fail when connectivity fails.

**Spontaneous meetings.** No calendar invite means no bot invite. But the conversation still matters.

**Phone-heavy roles.** Sales, customer success, recruiting—if many of your calls happen by phone, a speakerphone or Mac calling app setup brings them into the same notes as your video calls.

**Bot-averse counterparties.** Some clients refuse meetings with recording bots. Recording from your Mac avoids the bot, though you still need their consent.

## The legal side

Quick note on recording laws, because this matters:

Recording conversations has legal requirements that vary by jurisdiction. Some places require all-party consent. Others only require one-party consent. Some have specific rules for phone calls versus in-person conversations.

Cloud or local, the legal requirements are the same. The difference is what happens to the recording after:

- With cloud services, the recording sits on the provider's servers under its retention settings.
- With local recording, you decide what happens to the file.

Know your local laws. Get consent when required. Local processing doesn't change the legal obligations—it just gives you more control over the data.

## What you lose without the cloud

To be fair, local-only has trade-offs:

- **No automatic joining.** You manually start and stop recording. There's no bot that shows up when your calendar event starts.

- **Device-dependent.** Your Mac needs to be present and running. You can't send an AI to a meeting you're not attending.

- **No real-time collaboration.** Cloud services can share live transcripts with teammates. Local keeps everything on your device.

For many use cases, these trade-offs are fine. For others, a cloud service makes sense.

The point isn't that local is always better. The point is that local is now possible—and for conversations without a call to join, it keeps the recording on your Mac.
