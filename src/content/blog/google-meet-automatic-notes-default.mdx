On September 29, Google will begin rolling out a Google Meet setting that can turn on Gemini's “Take notes for me” automatically when a meeting reaches three participants.

For some Google Workspace editions, that new setting will be **on by default**. For others, it will be off. Users can override it, administrators can govern it, and automatic notes are not the same thing as automatic audio or video recording.

That narrower description matters. The change is significant, but the headline “Google Meet will record every meeting automatically” would be wrong.

This guide explains exactly what changes, which customers need to act before September 29, and why a product default is not a substitute for a consent policy. It provides general privacy and compliance information, not legal advice.

## What Google is changing

Google's [September 22 Workspace announcement](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/09/new-google-meet-take-notes-for-me-settings-for-admins-and-end-users-take-effect-September-29th.html) describes a new automatic note-taking preference. When enabled, “Take notes for me” starts after a third participant joins an eligible meeting.

The rollout is scheduled to begin on **September 29, 2026** and may take more than two weeks to reach everyone.

The initial defaults are split by edition:

| Workspace edition | Initial default for the new setting |
|---|---|
| Business Standard | On |
| Business Plus | On |
| Enterprise Standard | Off |
| Enterprise Plus | Off |
| Frontline Plus | Off |
| Google AI Pro for Education | Off |

An administrator can choose whether the feature is available. An end user can change the personal preference when the feature is available. Google says users will also receive a notification about the change.

The three-person threshold means the organizer and one guest can enter without triggering automatic notes. The feature becomes active when another person joins. That boundary is operational, not legal: the number of attendees does not determine whether notice or consent is required.

## Notes are not the same as a recording

Google Meet separates several capture features:

- **Take notes for me** uses Gemini to create meeting notes in a Google Doc.
- **Transcripts** create a written record of speech.
- **Recordings** preserve meeting audio and video.

Those artifacts can overlap, but they are not interchangeable. Turning on automatic notes does not, by itself, mean Meet is saving a conventional audio or video recording.

The privacy question is still real. To generate notes, the system has to process what people say. The resulting document can contain decisions, names, customer details, health information, employment matters, or other sensitive content. Google also says screenshots in notes are preselected unless an administrator disables that option.

For governance purposes, the better question is not only “Are we recording?” It is:

> What content is processed, what artifact is created, where is it stored, who receives it, and how long is it retained?

## What participants will see

Google's [Meet note-taking documentation](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/14754931?hl=en) says participants are notified when note-taking is active. The organizer can select who receives the notes, and the document is saved in Drive.

Google also offers an [explicit-consent control](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/04/require-explicit-consent-for-take-notes-with-Gemini-recordings-and-transcripts-in-Google-Meet.html) that can apply to note-taking, recordings, and transcripts. When an administrator enables it, participants must choose **Start**, **Join**, or **Continue** before entering or remaining in a captured session.

That control is off by default. It also includes an optional “Quick Stop” path, and the interface may preselect applying the user's decision to future instances of a recurring meeting.

These are useful product controls. They do not decide whether consent is legally sufficient in a particular jurisdiction or employment context. A click can document an interaction with the interface; it does not automatically cure a vague purpose, excessive retention, an invalid legal basis, or pressure on someone who cannot meaningfully refuse.

## The September 29 admin checklist

Workspace administrators should review the setting before rollout rather than wait for the first unexpected notes document.

### 1. Identify affected editions and users

Confirm which organizational units have Business Standard or Business Plus, because those are the editions where Google says the new preference starts on. Do not assume one domain-wide answer if licensing differs across subsidiaries, schools, or departments.

### 2. Choose an organizational default

Decide whether automatic note-taking is suitable for ordinary meetings. A sensible answer may differ for sales, engineering, recruiting, legal, HR, healthcare, or executive teams.

If exceptions matter, configure them through organizational units or groups where the Admin console permits it. Document who owns the policy and when it will be reviewed.

### 3. Evaluate explicit consent

Determine whether to require Google's explicit-consent flow for notes, transcripts, and recordings. Review the jurisdictions in which participants work or join, contractual confidentiality duties, works-council requirements, sector rules, and internal policies.

For interstate or international calls, use the stricter applicable standard unless counsel has approved a different rule. A host's physical location is not the only location that can matter.

### 4. Control screenshots

Screenshots can expose more than spoken words: slides, chat messages, attendee names, dashboards, account numbers, source code, or personal notifications. If screenshot capture is unnecessary, turn it off centrally rather than asking every host to remember.

### 5. Set sharing and retention

Define who should receive the generated Doc: the organizer, invited guests, actual attendees, or a smaller group. Apply Drive labels, data-loss prevention, access rules, and retention policies where available.

Meeting notes should not become permanent merely because nobody owns deletion.

### 6. Exclude sensitive meetings

Create clear rules for meetings that should not use automatic notes. Common exclusions include:

- disciplinary, termination, grievance, and accommodation meetings;
- legal advice and litigation strategy;
- incident response and security investigations;
- board or executive sessions;
- healthcare, counselling, or safeguarding discussions; and
- conversations involving particularly sensitive customer data.

An exclusion should be enforced by configuration or a runbook, not left as a vague reminder.

### 7. Tell users what changes

Explain the three-person trigger, where notes appear, who gets them, and how to stop the feature. Hosts also need a script for external guests and a way to continue the meeting when someone declines.

## A short host script

A notification banner is easy to miss, especially for dial-in participants or people joining late. A short spoken disclosure gives everyone the same context:

> “Gemini note-taking is on. It will create a shared notes document for [recipients] and we retain it for [period]. Please say so now if you do not want this meeting captured; we can turn it off and take manual notes.”

The script should match the actual configuration. Do not promise limited recipients if a calendar rule will email the document more broadly.

## Why defaults change behaviour

An opt-in tool requires a host to decide that notes are appropriate. A default-on tool reverses the burden: the host must notice and stop it when notes are inappropriate.

That difference is large in practice. Repeated meetings become habits. Attendees may infer that note-taking has been approved centrally. Generated documents accumulate in Drive. A recurring meeting can drift from routine updates into performance, health, legal, or customer-sensitive topics without anyone revisiting the original capture decision.

Good governance therefore needs **stop events**, not just start settings. Hosts should pause or end capture when the purpose, audience, or sensitivity changes.

## What local note-taking changes

A local workflow can avoid creating a shared cloud document by default. With Summit and Local Models selected, capture, transcription, speaker separation, summarisation, storage, and search can stay on the Mac. There is no meeting bot and no automatic Google Drive recipient list.

That is a narrower data path, not a legal exemption. The person using Summit still needs an appropriate basis to capture the conversation, must give required notice, and should protect the Mac, set retention, and avoid recording sensitive phases unnecessarily.

Summit's optional hosted summarisation and iCloud features create additional paths when enabled. The exact boundaries are described in the [security architecture](/security/architecture).

## The practical takeaway

The September 29 change is not “Google records every meeting.” It is a new automatic note-taking preference, initially enabled for Business Standard and Business Plus, that starts when a meeting reaches three people.

Admins should decide before rollout whether that is the right default, whether explicit consent should be required, whether screenshots should be disabled, and which meetings must remain outside the workflow. Hosts should treat every change in purpose or audience as a fresh capture decision.

The safest default is the one your organisation chose deliberately—not the one that arrived with a product update.
