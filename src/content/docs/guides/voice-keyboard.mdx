Voice Keyboard turns a short dictation into text and pastes it at the cursor in another app. Speech recognition runs locally using Parakeet, the model used by FluidAudio transcription. Dictation is separate from recording a meeting note.

## Set up Voice Keyboard

1. Open **Summit → Settings → Voice Keyboard**.
2. Turn **Voice keyboard** on.
3. Keep the default **Option–Space** shortcut, or click **Shortcut** and press your preferred key combination.
4. Click **Open Accessibility Settings…** and enable Summit if you want it to paste automatically into other apps.

![Voice Keyboard settings with shortcut, floating widget, and permission controls](/docs/screenshots/voice-keyboard.webp)

Microphone access is also required. If macOS asks for it on your first attempt, allow access and start dictating again. Voice Keyboard uses the microphone selected in **Settings → Recording**.

The Parakeet model downloads on first use if it is not already available. Finish that download before using dictation offline. **Language Mode** in **Settings → Speech Models** selects English-only or multilingual recognition. Voice Keyboard uses Parakeet even if you selected Whisper for meeting transcription.

## Hold to talk

1. Put the cursor in the app where you want the text.
2. Hold **Option–Space**, or your chosen shortcut, and speak.
3. Release the keys. Summit finishes recognition and pastes the text.

The text near the widget is a live preview. The final result is decoded after you finish and may differ from that preview.

## Dictate without holding a key

Tap the shortcut once to keep listening. Tap it again to finish and paste. Press **Esc** while listening to cancel without pasting.

A dictation stops automatically after five minutes and pastes what it recognized. For a long conversation you want to keep as a note, use [Record and Import Audio](/docs/guides/recording).

## Use the floating widget

Enable **Keep widget on screen** to leave the Summit logo visible. Hold it to talk, or click it once to keep listening and again to finish. Drag it to another position; use **Widget size** to resize it.

When **Keep widget on screen** is off, the widget appears only while you dictate. The keyboard shortcut still works.

## If the text is not pasted

Without Accessibility access, Summit leaves the recognized text on the clipboard and shows **Copied — press ⌘V to paste**. Return to your target app and press **Command–V** yourself.

For automatic pasting, enable Summit in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility**. Put the cursor in an editable field before finishing dictation. With automatic pasting, Summit restores the clipboard contents it temporarily replaced.

If the shortcut is already registered by another app, Voice Keyboard settings show an error. Choose a different combination. If your build reports that local transcription is unavailable, Voice Keyboard cannot be enabled in that build.
